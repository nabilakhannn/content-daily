# Writing SOP — Frameworks + 7-Layer Execution

Three decisions. In order: content type → hook formula → framework. Then execute with the 7 layers.

---

## STEP 0 — Content Type Selection (fires before everything else)

Look at your source material. This determines your hook formula AND your framework automatically.

| What you have | Content Type | Format | Goal |
|--------------|-------------|--------|------|
| Framework / tested system / research / how-to / step-by-step | **Educational** | Carousel or document | Authority + saves |
| Personal moment / story / vulnerability / emotion around a win | **Storytelling** | Text + candid photo | Trust + comments |
| Real result with specific number + system that produced it | **Lead Magnet** | Text + DM CTA | DMs + follows |
| Client or student win with before/after number | **Client Result** | Text | Social proof + DMs |
| Breaking trend or news + you have a genuine contrarian take | **Trend Jacking** | Text (publish fast) | Reach spike |
| Real interaction with known figure, brand, or framework | **Associations** | Text + screenshot | Positioning + reach |

**If you're not sure: default to Storytelling.** Trust builds faster than authority, and trust is what converts.

### Hook formulas by content type

**Educational:**
`[Time/effort invested in X] + [specific testing] + "Here's what actually works [qualifier]:"`
> "I spent 90 days testing every LinkedIn content format. Here's what actually works for coaches in 2026:"

**Storytelling:**
`[Dream achievement or milestone], but [unexpected obstacle / fear / vulnerability]`
> "I landed my biggest client ever, but nearly ghosted the DM for four days."

**Lead Magnet (Formula A — system reveal):**
`[Your system] is unbeatable. It [result] (even with [disqualifier people assume blocks them]).`
> "My content system runs on $40/month in AI tools. It replaced a $3,000/month ghostwriter. No agency. No team."

**Lead Magnet (Formula B — result + anti-feature):**
`[Bold result] in [time]. Without [assumption 1]. No [assumption 2].`
> "First 3 discovery calls booked. No cold outreach. No engagement pods. No paid ads."

## STEP 1 — Pick Your Framework

Framework determines the *architecture*. The 7 layers below are the execution.

### Framework A — SLAY (default)
**Use when:** story + lesson combined. Works for case studies, personal stories, client wins, behind-the-scenes.

| Layer | What it is | Word count |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| **S** — Story | Specific moment, not summary | 80-120 words |
| **L** — Lesson | What that moment taught you | 60-100 words |
| **A** — Actionable | The 3-5 step framework they can use | 80-120 words |
| **Y** — You / CTA | Invite comment or follow | 20-30 words |

Total: 250-400 words. Shorter = stronger with SLAY.

---

### Framework B — PAS
**Use when:** educational content about a problem. Works for myth-busting, common mistakes, "what most people miss" posts.

| Layer | What it is |
|-------|-----------|
| **P** — Problem | Name the problem in their language |
| **A** — Agitate | Show why it's worse than they think |
| **S** — Solution | Your take. ONE clear answer |

---

### Framework C — AIDA
**Use when:** authority play, lead magnet, or promoting something. High-stakes content. Think before using.

| Layer | What it is |
|-------|-----------|
| **A** — Attention | Hook that stops the scroll |
| **I** — Interest | Pull them deeper with a surprising angle |
| **D** — Decision | Make the case for your POV |
| **A** — Action | Clear CTA |

---

## Framework Selection Table

Content type → framework mapping (auto-flows from Step 0):

| Content Type | Framework | Post subtypes that fit |
|-------------|----------|----------------------|
| **Educational** | PAS | Myth-busting, common mistake, "what most people miss", thought leader opinion, "what I learned" |
| **Storytelling** | SLAY | Personal story, confession, behind-the-scenes, "what this moment taught me" |
| **Lead Magnet** | AIDA | Your result reveal, offer, authority play (credentials + proof), system reveal |
| **Client Result** | SLAY | Client case study, student win, before/after transformation |
| **Trend Jacking** | PAS | News reframe, industry event take, viral moment + your POV |
| **Associations** | SLAY | Met/heard known figure, mentioned by brand, shared belief with authority |

If source fits two types, use content type that matches your GOAL this week (trust-building week → Storytelling, authority week → Educational, pipeline week → Lead Magnet).

## Pre-Write Rule

**Only write from a validated hook.**

Before drafting anything, check: did someone already react to this idea in conversation, comments, or DMs? Resonance in real life = resonance on LinkedIn. If you're inventing a topic in the dark, it's a risk.

Also: use voice memos. Say the post out loud while driving or walking. If you can't say it naturally, you can't write it cleanly. Record → transcribe → clean up.

**30-minute rule:** if the post isn't flowing in 30 minutes, the idea isn't ready. Stop. Come back to it. Don't force structure onto a half-formed thought.

## Layer 1 — Hook (1-2 lines)
**Goal:** Stop the scroll in 1.5 seconds.

Rules:
- Max 12 words per line
- No vague claims ("Here's a thought")
- Specific > general (numbers, names, places)
- Trigger curiosity, contradiction, or emotion

Example:
> "I almost fired my best client last Tuesday."
> "Best decision I made this year."

---

## Layer 2 — Context (under 80 words)
**Goal:** Drop the reader into the scene.

Rules:
- ONE specific moment (not summary)
- Concrete details (when, where, who)
- No backstory, no setup
- Show don't tell

Example:
> "He paid $8K/month for 14 months. Always on time. Always polite. Then he forwarded my deliverable to his CMO with the line, 'see if this is worth it.' I read that and felt the floor drop."

---

## Layer 3 — Emotion (150-200 words)
**Goal:** Make the reader feel something specific.

Rules:
- Alternate internal (thoughts/feelings) + external (actions/dialogue)
- Sensory details (sound, sight, body sensations)
- Avoid abstract emotions ("I was sad") in favor of physical ("my chest tightened")
- Vulnerability invites engagement

Example:
> "I closed the laptop. Walked to the kitchen. Stared at the kettle for 4 minutes without turning it on..."

---

## Layer 4 — Value (150-200 words)
**Goal:** Deliver YOUR opinion + the contrast.

Rules:
- Common belief vs your truth
- Evidence (numbers, examples, lived experience)
- Take a position (not "it depends")
- Frame the lesson without preaching

Example:
> "Most agencies will tell you to fight for the client. Send the win-back email. Lower the price. I think that's backwards. The client who tests your worth in private is already gone..."

---

## Layer 5 — Rehook (~50 words at the 60% mark)
**Goal:** Pull the reader back if attention is fading.

Rules:
- One sentence that reframes what they just read
- Or pose the question they're now asking
- Whitespace before and after for visual reset

Example:
> "But here's the part nobody tells you about firing a client."

---

## Layer 6 — CTA (20-40 words)
**Goal:** Invite conversation, not a transaction.

Rules:
- Open-ended question
- Match the post's emotional energy
- Avoid "comment X for the link" tricks
- Avoid "DM me" unless the post earned it

Examples:
- "Have you ever fired a client who paid? What changed for you after?"
- "Curious — does this match what you're seeing in your space?"

---

## Layer 7 — Format (whitespace + flow)

Rules:
- Paragraphs under 40 words (most under 25)
- Break every 1-2 sentences for whitespace
- Total post: 800-1200 words
- No bullet points unless content is genuinely a list
- No headers (LinkedIn doesn't render them well in feed)
- No emojis at start of lines (looks templated)

---

## Specificity Rules (#1 principle)

Specificity builds trust faster than any other device. It's the shortcut.

**Weak → Strong replacements:**

| Weak (generic) | Strong (specific) |
|----------------|------------------|
| "Many clients" | "47 clients over 3 years" |
| "Recently" | "Last Tuesday" |
| "A lot of growth" | "3X'd their inbound in 6 weeks" |
| "Significant revenue" | "$14K in 11 days" |
| "Most businesses" | "Every single one of the 8 founders I spoke to last month" |
| "Some time ago" | "September 2024" |
| "Great results" | "First discovery call booked in 48 hours" |

**Number rule:** 9,999 is more credible than 10,000. Never round up. Use exact numbers even if they feel awkward.

**Time rule:** Always anchor to a specific moment. "This Monday," "Day 3," "In the first 10 minutes." Vague time = vague story.

**Name rule:** name people when you can (with permission). "A client named Raj" > "a client." Real names signal real stories.

## Format Selection — 2026 Rules

Per live testing across 100+ clients: **static photo + text beats video** on LinkedIn in 2026.

| Format | When to use |
|--------|------------|
| Static photo (you, your work, your environment) | Default for story + lesson posts |
| Text-only | Strong opinion / thought leadership |
| Carousel | Step-by-step frameworks with 5+ steps |
| Video | Reserved for high-emotion or announcement content only |

**Photo guidance:**
- Real photo of you > stock photo
- Candid > staged
- Environment that matches the post context (home office for systems content, client meeting for case studies)
- No generic handshake, lightbulb, or rocket images

## Credibility-Before-Claim Pattern

Open with proof of why you're saying this. Then make the claim.

**Pattern:** `[What you tested/did/experienced] → [What you found] → [Claim]`

**Weak:** "LinkedIn rewards specificity over follower count."

**Strong:** "I took on 100+ new clients in February. Tested every single theory live. Three hypotheses I thought were true were all wrong."

Credibility first. Claim second. The reader earns the right to believe you.

---

## Contrast Proof Pattern

Two characters. Different contexts. Same mechanism worked. Shows it's the *method*, not the person.

**Template:**
> "Greg has 30,000 followers. Alex has 2,000. Greg posts about private equity and goes viral weekly. Alex posted one post and his DMs haven't stopped. Both speak about one thing. LinkedIn doesn't reward size. It rewards specificity."

Use when: proving a principle works regardless of follower count, industry, experience level.

---

## Belief Shift Architecture (3-belief structure)

Every educational post should flip at least one belief. Best posts flip all three:

1. FROM: "[Common wrong belief]" → TO: "[Your truth]"
2. FROM: "[Fear or objection]" → TO: "[Reframe]"
3. FROM: "[Industry myth]" → TO: "[What actually works]"

Map these BEFORE writing. The belief shifts are the skeleton. The story/data fills them in.

---

## Anti-Patterns (BANNED — auto-fail)

Skill scans for these. If any present, rewrite + rescan:

- Em dashes (—) — use comma, period, or rewrite
- "It's not about X, it's about Y."
- Parallel triplet fragments: "Same price. Same quality. Same her."
- Stock corporate words: revolutionary, game-changing, skyrocket, leverage, unlock, unleash, dive deep
- "At the end of the day"
- "Buckle up / hot take / unpopular opinion"
- Sycophantic openers ("Great question," "Love this")
- Lists that always have exactly 3 items (vary the count)
- Paragraphs all the same length (vary length)
- Zero contractions (humans use don't / can't / won't)
- Zero hedging ("kinda", "I think", "honestly") — humans hedge

---

## Required human signals (must have at least 2)

- Self-correction ("Actually, scratch that —" or "Wait, that's not right.")
- Specific numbers (not "a lot" but "47 emails")
- Hedging ("I think", "maybe", "probably")
- Casual phrases ("look,", "honestly,", "real talk")
- Mid-thought reset (one short fragment after a long sentence)
